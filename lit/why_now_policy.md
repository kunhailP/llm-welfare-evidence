### 1a. ABAWD time limit (Sec. 10102; amends 7 U.S.C. 2015(o)) — Verified (opened)
- **Age range.** The ABAWD time limit used to apply from 18 to 54. The Fiscal Responsibility Act of 2023 (FRA) had been phasing the upper age up from 49 to 54. It now applies from **18 to 64**. The statute's wording is "under 18, or over 65, years of age" (new 6(o)(3)(A)). The FNS Oct 3 memo reads this as "individuals aged 18 to 64 are now subject to the time limit."
  - Encoding note: the statute says "over 65" and FNS says "18 to 64". Follow FNS, and mention the gap in a footnote if the gold labels depend on age 65.
  - The general work requirement exemption at age 60+ (Sec. 6(d)(3)) is unchanged. The definition of "elderly" (60+) is also unchanged, so people aged 60–64 are still "elderly" for the uncapped shelter deduction and the medical deduction, even though the ABAWD time limit now covers them.
- **Dependent-child exception.** It used to cover a household member responsible for a dependent child under 18. It now covers only a child **under 14**. Adults in households whose children are all aged 14–17 are now subject to the time limit.
- **Exceptions removed.** The FRA 2023 exceptions are gone: (1) homeless individuals, (2) veterans, (3) people aged 24 or younger who were in foster care on their 18th birthday.
- **Exceptions added.** "An Indian" or "Urban Indian" as defined in IHCIA §4(13) and §4(28), and "California Indian" under IHCIA §809(a).
- **Exceptions kept.** Medically certified unfit for work, exempt under 6(d)(2), and pregnant.
- **Effective date.** The changes took effect on enactment, **July 4, 2025**. States had to screen at initial application and recertification. FNS gave a 120-day QC variance exclusion (hold-harmless) that ended **Nov 1, 2025**.
- **Waivers.** The "insufficient jobs" waiver criterion was removed. An area can now get a waiver only if its unemployment rate is **over 10%**. Noncontiguous states (Alaska and Hawaii; Guam and the USVI are excluded) can qualify at ≥1.5× the national rate. A new "good faith" exemption for AK/HI expires no later than **Dec 31, 2028**. FNS said it would terminate non-qualifying waivers that relied on the old criterion (waivers memo, Oct 3, 2025).
- **Current core rule (unchanged).** Under 7 U.S.C. 2015(o)(2), an ABAWD who in the preceding **36-month** period received benefits for **3 months** without working or doing a work program for **20 hours/week averaged monthly** loses eligibility. 7 CFR 273.24(a)(1)(i) says "20 hours a week averaged monthly means **80 hours a month**." Sources: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/7/2015 (current text, reflects the OBBB) and eCFR 7 CFR 273.24 (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-7/subtitle-B/chapter-II/subchapter-C/part-273/subpart-G/section-273.24) — **Verified (opened)**

### 1b. Other OBBB eligibility and benefit changes relevant to gold computation — Verified (opened)
- **Sec. 10103, SUA and energy assistance.** Receiving more than $20/year in LIHEAP or similar energy assistance now confers the heating/cooling SUA **only for households with an elderly or disabled member**. Other households must actually incur heating or cooling costs. Third-party state energy assistance is treated differently depending on whether the household has an elderly or disabled member. Effective on enactment: new applicants at certification, ongoing cases at recertification at the latest.
- **Sec. 10104, internet.** Internet service fees are excluded from the excess shelter deduction and from SUA calculations. Effective on enactment.
- **Sec. 10101, Thrifty Food Plan.** The TFP is adjusted annually by CPI-U, and future re-evaluations cannot raise its cost. Effective Oct 1, 2025. The market basket cannot be re-evaluated before Oct 1, 2027.
- **Sec. 10108, noncitizens.** Eligibility is limited to citizens and nationals, LPRs, Cuban/Haitian entrants, and COFA residents. FNS issued a separate memo and Q&A, listed on https://www.fna.usda.gov/obbb. I did not open those, so the dates and details there are **Unverified**.
- **Sec. 10107, SNAP-Ed.** Funding ends after the FY2025 grant allocation.

### 1c. State benefit cost-share tied to payment error rate (Sec. 10105; new 7 U.S.C. 2013(a)(2)) — Verified (opened, statute)
| FY payment error rate (PER) | State share of benefit costs |
|---|---|
| < 6% | 0% |
| ≥ 6% and < 8% | 5% |
| ≥ 8% and < 10% | 10% |
| ≥ 10% | 15% |
- **Start.** FY2028 (from Oct 1, 2027). For FY2028 a state may elect its FY2025 **or** FY2026 PER. From FY2029 on, the PER from the third preceding fiscal year is used.
- **Delayed implementation.** If FY2025 PER × 1.5 ≥ 20% (that is, PER ≥ 13.33%), the state starts in FY2029. If FY2026 PER × 1.5 ≥ 20%, it starts in FY2030.
- **FY2025 PERs, released by USDA June 24, 2026.** National PER **10.62%** (overpayments 9.28%, underpayments 1.33%). QC tolerance threshold: $57. Source: https://www.fna.usda.gov/snap/qc/per ; PDF https://www.fna.usda.gov/sites/default/files/resource-files/snap-qcfy25-per.pdf — **Verified (opened)**
  - My count from that table, across 53 jurisdictions (50 states, DC, Guam, USVI): **10 below 6%, 6 at 6–8%, 16 at 8–10%, 21 at ≥10%**. Seven are at ≥13.33% and would qualify for the delay: Alaska 23.15, DC 18.66, New Mexico 16.81, Delaware 16.00, Georgia 15.21, Illinois 14.67, Oregon 14.14. (My own tabulation of the official table.)
- **Pending change (not law).** CRS Insight IN12723, "Senate Committee Chairman's New Farm Bill Draft Would Change the Upcoming State Sharing of SNAP Benefit Costs," Aug 4, 2026 (Gene Falk). Senate Ag Chairman Boozman's July 31, 2026 draft farm bill would delay cost-sharing by one year to **FY2029**, based on FY2026 PERs, and would raise the top tier to **20%** for PER ≥10% from FY2031. The House-passed 2026 farm bill (H.R. 7567) has no such provision. CRS also notes that USDA had "yet to issue regulations or guidance on how state cost-sharing of SNAP will be implemented." https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/IN/PDF/IN12723/IN12723.1.pdf — **Verified (opened)**. Whether this draft was marked up or enacted after Aug 2026 is **Unverified**; check before submission.

### 1d. State administrative cost share (Sec. 10106; 7 U.S.C. 2025(a)) — Verified (opened)
- The federal share of state administrative costs falls from 50% (through FY2026) to **25% from FY2027**, which begins Oct 1, 2026. The state share therefore goes from 50% to **75%**. This is already in effect as of this writing.

**Why it matters for the paper.** States now carry direct financial liability for eligibility errors (PER, from FY2028) and pay 75% of administrative costs (from Oct 2026). At the same time, the eligibility rules got more complex: ABAWD ages up to 64, the child-age cutoff at 14, tribal-status exceptions, and SUA tied to elderly/disabled status. That combination pushes states toward automation and AI just as rule-fidelity errors become expensive.

### 1e. Related: Medicaid work requirements (Sec. 71119; new SSA §1902(xx)) — Verified (opened, statute)
- Applies to individuals aged 19–64. They must have 80 hours/month of work, community service, or a work program, or half-time education. States must comply "not later than the first day of the first quarter that begins after **December 31, 2026**" (i.e., Jan 1, 2027), or earlier at state option. Good-faith exemptions expire no later than Dec 31, 2028. This is the second OBBB rule-change wave that states say they will handle with AI (see §4).

### 2a. Tests (Verified, FNA eligibility page)
- **Gross income test.** ≤130% FPL. Households with an elderly (60+) or disabled member only have to meet the net test.
- **Net income test.** ≤100% FPL.
- **Assets.** $3,000, or $4,750 (FY2027) with a member aged 60+ or disabled. For FY2026 the figures were $3,000 and $4,500. Excluded: home and lot, resources of SSI and TANF recipients, most retirement plans. Vehicle rules: FMV over $4,650 counts, plus an equity test.
- **BBCE.** "Most state agencies have adopted broad-based categorical eligibility" and align income and asset limits with TANF/MOE non-cash benefits. Under BBCE the federal asset test effectively does not apply in most states. Propel reports that "only 13 states still impose asset limits" (**Partial**: Propel blog, not FNS).
- **Benefit formula.** Allotment = max allotment − 0.3 × net income, with net income rounded up. A minimum benefit applies to 1–2 person households.

### 2b. Deductions (Verified, FNA eligibility page)
- 20% of earned income
- Standard deduction (by household size)
- Dependent care, when needed for work, training, or education
- Medical expenses over $35/month for elderly or disabled members
- Legally owed child support (in some states it is an income exclusion instead)
- Homeless shelter deduction
- Excess shelter: shelter costs above 50% of income after other deductions. Capped unless the household has an elderly or disabled member. Allowable costs include heating and cooking fuel, electricity, water, basic fee for one telephone, rent or mortgage, and property taxes. **Not internet** (OBBB Sec. 10104).

## 3. EU AI Act: public assistance eligibility is high-risk

- **Annex III point 5(a)** (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689). High-risk: "AI systems intended to be used by public authorities or on behalf of public authorities to evaluate the eligibility of natural persons for essential public assistance benefits and services, including healthcare services, as well as to grant, reduce, revoke, or reclaim such benefits and services." I read this on the Commission's AI Act Service Desk (consolidated text as at 27 July 2026): https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/annex-3 — **Verified (opened)**. Official OJ text: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj (EUR-Lex served a bot challenge to my fetcher, so I verified via the Commission's consolidated text).
- **Original application date** for high-risk obligations: Art. 113 originally set general application from **2 Aug 2026**, with Annex III high-risk obligations from that date. Verified on the Commission service desk Art. 113 page, which still states "It shall apply from 2 August 2026."
- **Digital Omnibus on AI ("AI Omnibus").** **ADOPTED and IN FORCE.** It moved the Annex III date.
  - Commission proposal: **19 Nov 2025**. Political agreement: **7 May 2026**. Entry into force: **27 July 2026**. Source: Commission page https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai, which links the final text at https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=OJ:L_202601744, i.e. **Regulation (EU) 2026/1744** — **Verified (opened)**
  - Amended Art. 113(c): Chapter III Sections 1–3 apply from **(i) 2 Dec 2027** for Annex III high-risk systems (so **benefits eligibility AI is now covered from 2 Dec 2027**) and from **(ii) 2 Aug 2028** for Annex I product-embedded systems. Source: https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-113 and the Commission timeline https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/timeline/timeline-implementation-eu-ai-act — **Verified (opened)**
  - Council final adoption on **29 June 2026**: press release https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2026/06/29/artificial-intelligence-council-gives-final-green-light-to-simplify-and-streamline-rules/ — **Partial**. Consilium returned 403. The date comes from search snippets and law-firm summaries, and it is consistent with the Commission timeline.
  - European Parliament plenary approval on 16 June 2026, signature on 8 July 2026, OJ publication on 24 July 2026: **Partial**. These come from law-firm and secondary summaries (e.g., K&L Gates, White & Case, Usercentrics). The 27 July entry-into-force date is verified on the Commission page.
- **Framing for the paper.** The Annex III benefits-eligibility obligations (risk management, data quality, logging, human oversight, accuracy and robustness, Art. 8–15) apply from 2 Dec 2027. Systems procured or built now will face these obligations within about one deployment cycle, and benchmarks that measure rule-faithful eligibility reasoning are directly relevant to the Art. 15 "accuracy" requirement. That last link is our argument, not a legal finding.

## 4. Deployments, pilots, and governance of AI/LLMs in benefits administration (2024–2026)

### 4a. US federal governance
- **OMB M-24-10** (Mar 28, 2024, Young), *Advancing Governance, Innovation, and Risk Management for Agency Use of AI*. Appendix I(2), "Purposes That Are Presumed to Be Rights-Impacting," item (l): "Making decisions regarding access to, eligibility for, or revocation of critical government resources or services; ... detecting fraudulent use or attempted use of government services; assigning penalties in the context of government benefits." https://bidenwhitehouse.archives.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/M-24-10-Advancing-Governance-Innovation-and-Risk-Management-for-Agency-Use-of-Artificial-Intelligence.pdf — **Verified (opened)**
- **OMB M-25-21** (Apr 3, 2025, Vought), *Accelerating Federal Use of AI through Innovation, Governance, and Public Trust*. It **rescinds and replaces M-24-10**. AI is "high-impact" when "its output serves as a principal basis for decisions or actions that have a legal, material, binding, or significant effect on rights or safety," and this applies whether or not a human oversees the decision.
  - Sec. 6(m) presumes high-impact: "Ability to apply for, or adjudication of, requests for critical federal services, processes, and benefits ...; determination of continued eligibility for ongoing benefits; ... detection of fraudulent use ...; adjudication of penalties in the context of government benefits."
  - Agencies had **365 days** (to about Apr 2026) to document minimum practices for high-impact AI.
  - https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/M-25-21-Accelerating-Federal-Use-of-AI-through-Innovation-Governance-and-Public-Trust.pdf — **Verified (opened)**. Note: OMB memos bind federal agencies, not state SNAP agencies.
- **USDA FNS, "Use of Advanced Automation in SNAP"**, revised **Feb 24, 2025** (original Jan 2024; S. Gersten-Paal). https://www.usda.gov/sites/default/files/guidance-documents/fns.snap-automation-revised-022425.pdf — **Verified (opened)**
  - RPA, bots, and virtual agents that replace merit-staff functions count as "major changes" (7 CFR 272.15).
  - States must **request FNS approval** for AI or ML and other advanced automation beyond the listed examples, because "these types of advanced automation technologies may introduce bias or errors into eligibility determinations." Requests must explain bias mitigation, effects on access and merit staff, audit procedures supporting appeals, and ongoing accuracy and equity monitoring.
  - Acknowledged examples: AZ voice virtual agent for periodic reports; GA voice and digital agents plus RPA that pre-populates recertifications for merit-worker review; IL RPA for no-change periodic reports; KY chatbot, OCR, and virtual agent; MA virtual agent; NM RPA bots ("BabyBot", "UpdateBot"); OH chatbot answering "case status, benefit amount, and eligibility"; TX RPA issuing adverse-action notices to one-person households reported deceased.

## 5. Documented LLM errors in eligibility or benefits advice (2024–2026)

- **Gosciak, Giannella, Guo, Chen, Koenecke, "LLMs in social services: How does chatbot accuracy affect human accuracy?"** arXiv:2603.11213, 11 Mar 2026. A 770-question SNAP-style benchmark plus a randomized experiment with LA nonprofit caseworkers. Control accuracy was 49%. High-quality chatbot suggestions (96–100% accurate) raised caseworker accuracy by 27 points. Incorrect suggestions cut accuracy by about two-thirds on easy questions. The authors describe an "AI underreliance plateau." https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11213 — **Verified (opened)**. This is **closely related prior work**; position the paper against it.
- **Propel, "Building a SNAP LLM eval," parts 1–3** (Apr–Jun 2025). On an illustrative set of 25 SNAP cases: GPT-4o mini 48%, o3-mini 56%, Claude 3.5/3.7 Sonnet 68%, o1 76%, Gemini 2.0 Flash and 2.5 Pro 80%. Many models gave **prior-FY income limits** when tested in Feb 2025. Answers to state-dependent asset-limit questions (TX vs CA) varied widely, with refusals and errors. https://www.propel.app/insights/building-a-snap-llm-eval-part-1/ , https://www.propel.app/insights/building-a-snap-llm-eval-part-2-testing-and-automation/ , https://www.propel.app/insights/building-a-snap-llm-eval-part-3-testing-nuanced-capabilities/ — **Verified (opened)**. These are an industry blog, not peer-reviewed.
- **Nava Labs (Jul 2024).** RAG chatbot accuracy on SNAP navigator questions was "highly variable" (see §4b). **Verified.**
- **Nevada UI appeals GenAI.** In testing it cited an incorrect Nevada law and missed appeal documents (see §4b). **Verified.**
- **NYC MyCity chatbot** (Microsoft-powered). The Markup and THE CITY (29 Mar 2024) found it said landlords need not accept **Section 8 housing vouchers**, which is illegal source-of-income discrimination. Ten staffers asked the same question and all got the wrong answer. The Markup (30 Jan 2026) reported that Mayor Mamdani's administration would take it down; an update says it was removed by 4 Feb 2026. https://themarkup.org/news/2024/03/29/nycs-ai-chatbot-tells-businesses-to-break-the-law , https://themarkup.org/artificial-intelligence/2026/01/30/mamdani-to-kill-the-nyc-ai-chatbot-we-caught-telling-businesses-to-break-the-law — **Verified (opened)**
- **US Senators Warren, Schumer, and Hirono (2 Apr 2025)** opened an investigation into DOGE's plan to replace Education Department student-aid call centers with a GenAI chatbot, citing hallucination risk, including misleading students about **Pell Grant eligibility**. https://www.warren.senate.gov/newsroom/press-releases/warren-schumer-hirono-open-investigation-into-doges-ai-chatbot-plan-to-replace-education-department-call-centers — **Verified (opened)**
